# S10 Channels — status (2026-10-19)

PRs: devmello/psyche#10 (`protocol:` channel additions) and the `[s10 channels]` PR from `ws/s10-channels` (stacked on #10).

## What works (M1)

- **Framework (N1).** `ChannelService` provides:
  - adapter registry, start with reconnect backoff, and health-check restarts;
  - an inbound pipeline: authorize, then pair / command / route, then `InboundSink`;
  - Markdown rendering to plain text or Telegram HTML;
  - chunking by rendered length, with code fences re-opened across chunks;
  - per-chat and per-channel token buckets, plus `rate_limited` retry;
  - typing indicators, and Library ingestion of attachments.
- **Authorization (N2).**
  - `dmPolicy`: `pairing` (default), `allowlist`, `open` (needs `"*"`), `disabled`.
  - Pairing codes: 8 characters, 1 h, at most 3 pending per channel, re-sent at most every 10 min. Approve or deny via the API; an approved sender joins `allowFrom`.
  - Owner via `ownerIds` and `commands.ownerAllowFrom`. Weak senders are never owners. Identity links, with one identity per sender.
  - Groups: group allowlist, `groupAllowFrom`, and `requireMention` from structured mentions.
  - `contextVisibility`, and command authorization.
- **Routing.**
  - `dmScope` session keys; non-owners never share `main`; identity-linked per-peer sessions; `injectMemory` only in the owner's private sessions.
  - Provenance parts: forwarded text is untrusted; quoted text keeps its author's provenance; edits are delivered as `edit`. `ChannelInboundEvent.taintSources` names where untrusted content came from (#275, channels part); S01 must start the turn tainted with them (the Gateway part: `turnText` flattens parts and `sessions.send` carries no provenance).
- **Approvals over chat (N4).**
  - Telegram buttons, or `/approve <ref> approve|deny` as a fallback.
  - Owner-only answers, always scope `once`; broker errors map to short replies.
  - Restart-invalidated and replayed answers are rejected.
  - Sanitized fixed-template cards; a card that does not fit becomes a pointer to a device.
  - **Cards per surface (design-12, closed 2026-10-19 on `ws/cards-per-surface`).** Every chat card is `renderForSurface("<channel>:<peerId>", approval)` from `@psyche/permissions/cards` plus the chat trailer (expiry, `/approve` ref), in `ChatApprovals.deliver` and `TelegramAdapter.sendApprovalButtons`. A `deviceOnly`, financial, forbidden-kind or non-opted-in `destructive` approval gets the header and "Answer this on your Mac." only (no arguments, no buttons, no ref), and is never answerable from the channel. Tests: `test/format.test.ts` (secret placeholder, untrusted snippet, device-only, credential fill) and `test/approvals.test.ts`. Question cards are not delivered to channels yet (`renderQuestionForSurface` is ready for the iMessage / M2 work).
- **WebChat (N3)** adapter. **Telegram (N4)** adapter on grammY 1.46.0, tested against hand-recorded Bot API fixtures.
- **Allowlisted senders (#293, channels part).** `buildRoute` gives `role: "allowed"` routes `toolAllow: []` (chat-only), so the requester the Gateway passes to the broker carries an empty tool list. The broker side (honour `toolAllow` / refuse reads for `allowed`, unpin `imsg-nonowner-read-chats-004`) is S05's.
- **Designated person mode (N10).** Persona overlay, tool allowlist, no `MEMORY.md`, own session, `grantSubject` for S05, approvals to the owner. Designated groups bind their member list.
- **Voice notes (N11).** Inbound voice is transcribed through `SpeechEngine`. Outbound text is synthesized once and transcoded once per container (OGG/Opus, CAF, MP3).
- **API.** `channels.*` handlers. `SqliteChannelStore` plus a migration for `channels` and `identities`.
- **Tests.** 151 vitest tests (19 of them iMessage), including regressions for the 10 findings of the pre-PR security review (`pnpm --filter @psyche/channels test`), no network.

## N5 iMessage (M2, `ws/m2-imessage`, on the protocol PR #208)

- **What works.** `IMessageAdapter` (`src/adapters/imessage/`) over the mac helper's `messages.*` node capabilities, through the same `ChannelService` pipeline as Telegram: `dmPolicy` / pairing, owner ids, identity links, designated persons and untrusted non-owner text behave identically. Inbound is one `messages.watch` from a persisted chat.db cursor (first start at the newest row; resumed without replays; saved on receipt, at most once), with per-chat FIFO dispatch. Own sends (`isFromMe`, including our echoes) and tapbacks are never turns. SMS / RCS / unknown handles are weak and never the owner. Groups are off unless listed; with no mentions in chat.db, `requireMention` groups activate only on inline replies to Psyche. Outbound text goes only into the conversation being answered, chunked at 4000 characters, plain text, no typing. Approval cards render through `renderForSurface("imessage:<peerId>", …)` with the `/approve` fallback and 👍 / 👎 tapbacks (owner only, strong identity, same 1:1 chat, scope `once`). Device-only approvals get the header and "Answer this on your Mac." only. A missing Full Disk Access grant reports "Grant Full Disk Access to Psyche Helper …" and backs off without opening new watches.
- **Outbound authorization (the rule).** Telegram replies are not broker-gated: the reply in the sender's own chat is the channel. iMessage keeps that rule for the recipient (only the conversation being answered), but `messages.send` is a destructive node capability that speaks as the Mac's Apple ID, and the node requires an `approvalId`. The adapter therefore asks an explicit port, `authorizeSend({channel, account, recipient, bodySha256, bindingHash, grantKey, purpose})`, for the id of an approved `message_send` approval (scope `once`) whose binding is `bindingFor("message_send", {channel: "imessage", account, recipient, bodySha256})`. It never invents an id; a refusal sends nothing. Tests back the port with a `FakePermissionBroker` and check `verifyBinding`.
- **Pairing confirmation (#277).** `ChannelServiceOptions.onPairingDecided` fires when the owner approves or denies a pairing request, before "You're approved" is sent. The Gateway's `ChannelConversations.pairingDecided` turns that sender's pairing DM (1:1 only, same channel and account) from `allows: "pairing_code"` into `allows: "reply"`, so the confirmation passes the reply authority as a reply to a now-routed sender. A denial changes nothing. The record is memory-only, so an approval after a Gateway restart is still refused until the sender writes again (fails closed).
- **Gateway.** `composeChannels(services, {imessage: {account, authorizeSend, nodeId?, cursor?}})` registers the adapter (cursor file under `$PSYCHE_HOME/channels/`); it starts only while a live node offers `messages.watch`, and otherwise retries with backoff. Nothing configures it by default.
- **Tests.** `test/imessage/` (19, on `FakeMessagesNode`): owner routed, non-owner untrusted, SMS weak never owner, pairing code under an approval bound to the chat, designated person `grantSubject`, identity-link sessions, echo dropped, unknown conversation refused, chunking, cursor resume, FDA health and backoff, retryable watch errors, groups and attachments, `/approve` from the owner and refused for a non-owner, tapbacks, device-only pointer, refused authorization.
- **Open.**
  - **S05 / S01 (done by S01):** the broker-issued reply authority is `PsychePermissionBroker.authorizeChannelReply({channel, account, recipient, bodySha256, bindingHash?, purpose})`. It issues an already-decided `message_send` approval (scope `once`, 5 min, `decidedBy: {channel, senderId}`, audited `approval.channel_reply`, never a card), but only in these cases: a `reply` to a conversation a routed sender wrote in within the last hour; a `pairing_code` to a fresh pairing conversation (5 min); an `approval_card` to the owner's own direct chat. It refuses everything else (audited `approval.channel_reply_refused`) and caps sends at 30 per conversation per minute. The channel layer supplies the conversations through `ChannelServiceOptions.onInboundDecision` → the Gateway's `ChannelConversations` (memory only). The composition root's default `authorizeSend` passes the replying run as `turn` (the conversation its request came from; the thread's mode; the thread's carried taint plus `tool_call.updated` taint flags; the requester), so a tainted turn writing to a new conversation, plan mode and deny rules are refused (security §3.3, §3.6). Because the helper only checks that an id is present, `verifiedMessagesSends` re-verifies and spends the approval at dispatch against the actual `messages.send` recipient and text, and records a `node.messages.send` receipt. `PSYCHE_IMESSAGE_ACCOUNT` turns the channel on. Telegram sends stay ungated (no send port).
  - **S14 mac helper:** attachment bytes (`fetchAttachment`) for inbound voice bubbles and images; outbound attachments / CAF voice; polls on macOS 26+.
  - Question cards (`renderQuestionForSurface`) are still not delivered to channels.

## Doc conflicts (I followed the stricter reading)

1. **Chat approval of `destructive`.** Architecture §4.6 says chat approves `write` only; security §9 says `write` and `destructive` (review design-04). Default here: `write` only. A channel can opt in with `config.allowDestructiveApprovals`. Note that `FakePermissionBroker` marks destructive as `deviceOnly` anyway.
2. **N4 "done when: the owner approves a purchase from Telegram"** contradicts security §3.7 / §9, where `financial`, purchase and credential fill are `deviceOnly`. Security wins: purchases are never delivered to or answered from chat, and `config.ownerDevice` does not change that. The feature spec row should be amended to say "approves an email send".
3. **"Ownership does not grant chat access"** (security §9) means an owner id must also pass `dmPolicy`. The owner pairs once, or is listed in `allowFrom`.
