# Meta "Muse" personal AI agent app (2026): product teardown notes

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope: Meta Platforms' consumer agent app "Muse" (iOS/Android/web/Mac/iPad/WhatsApp, launched Sept 8, 2026), its UI, personalization, agentic/permission UX, lineage from the Meta AI app, open-source look-alikes, and reception.

Disambiguation: this is Meta's "Muse" (model family from Meta Superintelligence Labs: Muse Spark, Muse Image/Video, Muse Glimmer, Muse Wonder; agent app "Muse"; internal codename "Hatch"). It is NOT Microsoft's Muse game-world model, the Muse writing/whiteboard apps, Adobe Muse, MuseScore, Muse Dash, the Muse EEG headband, or Hugging Face's text-to-image "open-muse" repo (several of those surfaced in searches and were discarded).

Sourcing caveats: Meta's official pages used were the newsroom launch post, the design write-up at introducing.muse.ai, the security/architecture post (security.muse.ai redirects to research.meta.ai), the Muse Spark posts, and platform.muse.ai. Pages that could not be fetched (HTTP 403/451/timeouts): CNBC (Oct 7), NPR (Sept 30), Axios (Sept 8 and Sept 25), Forbes (Sept 30), CNN hands-on (Sept 23; its findings are cited via Yahoo/Agent Sonic re-reporting), TechRadar zero-day article body, BetaNews Mac article. The Verge, Wired, Mobbin and Figma could not be checked because the shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-task. No primary Meta Help Center page was reachable (help.muse.ai does not resolve); two third-party guides that paraphrase Help Center text are used and flagged.

## Q0. What is Muse, and how does it relate to the Meta AI app and the Muse Spark model (dates, platforms, pricing)?

### Takeaway
Muse is a new, separate consumer "personal AI agent" app that Meta launched on September 8, 2026 (US; iOS, Android, muse.ai web, WhatsApp), followed by a Mac app (Sept 17/18), Canada, and an iPad app (Oct 7). It runs on the Muse Spark model family (first released April 8, 2026) inside a per-user cloud Linux VM; it is free with a weekly token cap plus $20 "Power" and $100 "Maximum" tiers. It is not a rename of the Meta AI app, which continues to exist in parallel.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's launch post (dated Sept 8, 2026, updated Sept 30) says Muse "is rolling out in the US on iOS, Android, and muse.ai, and coming soon to AI glasses," is also reachable through WhatsApp, is "free for most of what people need, with subscription plans for people who want to do more," and is "Powered by Muse Spark," which Meta calls its most capable model to date, built for real-world agentic work — [Meta Newsroom: Introducing Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)

- Muse is positioned as the successor platform ("centerpiece") rather than a rename; the Meta AI app absorbed agentic features in July–August 2026 and the two coexist, with Muse being the per-user-VM, always-on agent and Meta AI the broad chat assistant embedded in Meta's social apps.
- Pricing is a token-metered compute model (weekly pools), not a feature-gated one; a clone should expose a visible "usage remaining" meter rather than feature tiers.

### Gaps

## Q1. Exactly what tabs/screens exist in Muse and what is on each (layout, element order, labels)?

### Takeaway
On phones Muse is a five-tab messaging-style app (main Chat, Feed, Ideas, Goals, Library/media), with a top-left thread list (main chat + side chats), a tappable agent avatar at the top of the chat that opens an activity/approvals/upcoming/identity sheet, and a Settings area (gear on iPhone; bottom-left menu on Mac/web) holding Connectors, Permissions, Data controls and subscription. The Mac/web app uses a left rail with Chat, Search, Feed, Ideas, Goals, Library. Sources disagree slightly on tab names across builds (one Help-Center-derived guide lists only Chats, Goals, Library plus an Assistant icon).

### Cited Findings
Navigation skeleton
- Mobile bottom bar, in order: "main chat, a news-style feed, a light-bulb icon for ideas, a check-mark icon for goals, and a media folder for generated images"; top left opens chat threads "including a main chat and side chats"; top right three dots open the phone's files; the App Store logo is "a small squiggle" — [Creator walkthrough summary, Oct 9 2026](https://openclawdatabase.com/news/videos/2026-10-09-meta-muse-setup-walkthrough-gmail-calendar-browser-soul-memory/index.md)
- "On mobile, the five tabs sit at the bottom of the screen"; the five are Chat ("Where you talk to your agent. Everything starts here"; "looks like texting a person"; example prompts "Plan my week" and "Find me a cheaper flight to Chicago"), Feed ("Short posts your agent writes for you on a schedule"), Ideas ("Tap one and it builds it"), Goals ("Things you are working toward"), Library ("documents, images, files" the agent makes); on Mac "a menu button (two bars, bottom left) opens three options, of which only Settings is named"; Settings is "the gear icon" on iPhone and "the menu at the bottom of the left rail" on web — [Signal Drifts](https://signaldrifts.substack.com/p/how-to-muse-the-ai-agent-that-does)
- Mac window sidebar: Chat ("conversations, with side chats for separate tasks"), Search ("finds things within Muse"), Feed ("updates. A new account may need to generate its first feed"), Ideas ("suggested workflows and tasks"), Goals ("context about what you want to accomplish"), Library ("documents, artifacts, and media you've sent or received"); bottom-left menu > Settings includes Computer use, File system access, Permissions, Devices, and Data controls (with a "Help improve our AI models" option) — [The Rundown Mac setup guide](https://app.therundown.ai/guides/how-and-why-to-set-up-meta-muse-desktop)
- A Help-Center-derived guide instead lists main tabs "Chats, Goals and Library," plus an "Assistant icon" leading to "Upcoming (reminders and scheduled tasks) and Identity (Memory, Soul and Identity files)"; side chats are deleted individually from a three-dot menu in the Chats tab; Goals deleted from a three-dot menu with confirmation; Library has a "System Files" section holding "all files you uploaded or Muse created" — [saascrmreview](https://saascrmreview.com/how-to-use-meta-muse/)
- Another tutorial: the app "opens on the Chat tab"; "A menu icon in the top left opens a pencil-and-note icon for side chats"; the Assistant icon holds "Upcoming (reminders and scheduled tasks) and the Activity log"; Settings includes Permissions, Connectors, and Data controls ("Import memory" and the training toggle) — [AI Agents Library](https://www.aiagentslibrary.com/blog/how-to-use-meta-muse/)

- A security-focused explainer lists product surfaces by function: "A continuing main conversation plus focused side conversations," Goals, Ideas "with controls over proactive behavior," an "Artifact workspace" (documents, PDFs, web pages, dashboards outside the chat stream), and an "Activity view" that "Shows progress and approval requests" — [rywalker](https://rywalker.com/research/meta-muse)
- MindStudio's description: main chat, side chats, Feed ("surfaces proactive suggestions and ideas"), Goals ("objectives organized by category"), Artifacts, Activity log, "Approvals queue: items awaiting the user's sign-off," Upcoming (recurring tasks), "Identity area: shows what the agent has stored in its memory" — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/meta-muse-ai-agent)

### Inferences
- The most consistent model across sources is: (1) a persistent main chat with optional side chats (thread list top-left), (2) five destination tabs, (3) an "agent sheet" reached by tapping the avatar (activity log → tasks → per-task actions; approvals queue; upcoming/scheduled; identity files), (4) Settings with Connectors/Permissions/Data controls/Wallet/Secure store. A desktop clone should put tabs in a left rail (as the Mac app does) and the agent sheet behind the avatar in the chat header.
- The "Chats, Goals and Library" variant likely reflects either an earlier build or Help Center naming; the Feed and Ideas tabs are confirmed by multiple hands-on sources (Lenny's, The Rundown, Signal Drifts, the Oct 9 walkthrough).
- Iconography that is documented: light-bulb (Ideas), check-mark (Goals), fingerprint (Soul & memory), pencil (edit avatar/name), two-bar hamburger (Mac bottom-left menu), pencil-and-note (new side chat), three-dot overflow menus.

### Gaps
- No source describes colors, typography, exact spacing, or the pixel layout of individual screens; Meta's screenshot set is uncaptioned. iPad layout (sidebar vs. tab bar) is undocumented beyond "text-heavy interface."
- The WhatsApp surface's UX (how to start a Muse chat, what is unavailable there) was not described by any fetched source.

## Q2. How does Muse present the feed and how is it personalized (signals, controls, custom instructions)?

### Takeaway
The Feed tab is a private, agent-authored "news-style" stream of short posts (briefings, reminders, suggestions) generated on a schedule from the user's goals, memory and connected data; users can heart/comment, tell the agent what to write about, and dial proactivity up or down. It is a "generative UI" surface: fixed card structure, model-generated content. Precise ranking signals are not publicly documented.

### Cited Findings
- Feed = "Short posts your agent writes for you on a schedule. Content includes news briefings and reminders. 'You control what it writes about.'" — [Signal Drifts](https://signaldrifts.substack.com/p/how-to-muse-the-ai-agent-that-does)
- "A news-style feed 'tailored to you.' Users can heart posts and comment." — [Creator walkthrough](https://openclawdatabase.com/news/videos/2026-10-09-meta-muse-setup-walkthrough-gmail-calendar-browser-soul-memory/index.md)
- Cornell describes "generative UI": the structure stays fixed ("avatar, feed items, idea cards") while the content changes ("status emoji, personalized suggestions, small animations") — [Lenny's: How Meta designed Muse](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-ai-how-meta-designed-muse-with); iMasters: "a personalized ideas surface and a generative feed, built from each person's history and context," and testing shifts to "a different kind of rigor" because layouts vary each session — [iMasters](https://imasters.com/design-ux/how-meta-designed-muse-to-not-feel-like-an-ai-app)
- "Feed: updates. A new account may need to generate its first feed." — [The Rundown](https://app.therundown.ai/guides/how-and-why-to-set-up-meta-muse-desktop)
- Claire Vo's review chapter: "News feed: a personalized feed she configured" — [Lenny's review](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/muse-review-the-personal-ai-agent)
- Feed "surfaces proactive suggestions and ideas" — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/meta-muse-ai-agent)
- Proactivity control: "Muse can message unprompted, so the bar is high. Users can turn proactivity off, lower it, or raise it." Muse "notifies the user only when results are meaningfully new or need input." — [Meta: How We Designed Muse](https://introducing.muse.ai/)
- Personalization signals (Meta): Muse learns from conversations and "reflecting on what matters to them"; example: turns a saved Instagram recipe reel into a grocery list and remembers friends' dietary restrictions — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/); Engadget's test "turned saved Instagram and Facebook posts into organized recipe and restaurant collections" — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2281289/openai-dot-vs-meta-muse-ai-agents-comparison)
- Facebook, Instagram and Threads connect automatically when they share an Accounts Center ("This includes access to posts and DMs") — [Learn AI with Mariah](https://learnaiwithmariah.com/guides/meta-muse-6-things-to-try/); "Facebook and Instagram were connected by default during testing" — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/meta-muse-ai-agent)
- Background generation machinery (from the exported runtime): an hourly memory job, a nightly "dream" that "Reviews recent conversations and writes guidance under `~/dreams/`," and other jobs that "Maintain relationship pages, review recurring workflows, and prepare ideas or goal briefings" — [mouse.dev teardown](https://mouse.dev/blog/muse-runtime-export/); a task-log step labelled "adding cron" means Muse was setting up a schedule — [Creator walkthrough](https://openclawdatabase.com/news/videos/2026-10-09-meta-muse-setup-walkthrough-gmail-calendar-browser-soul-memory/index.md)
- TIME's reading of internal instructions: "Each hour, Muse updates dossiers on the user and on people mentioned," agents "run a nightly analysis of the day's conversations," and identify which prompts work best (e.g., "This user responds better to short nudges after 10 PM") — [TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/10/06/meta-muse-ai-agent-privacy/)
- Custom instructions equivalent: the Soul file ("personality and style instructions, such as concise, funny, or flowery") and Memory file ("facts and preferences about you") — [Creator walkthrough](https://openclawdatabase.com/news/videos/2026-10-09-meta-muse-setup-walkthrough-gmail-calendar-browser-soul-memory/index.md); "The Soul file changes how the agent behaves, not just what it knows" — [saascrmreview](https://saascrmreview.com/how-to-use-meta-muse/)
- Recurring briefings as feed-like content: a recurring weekday audio briefing and "longer personal podcasts" — [Learn AI with Mariah](https://learnaiwithmariah.com/guides/meta-muse-6-things-to-try/); a daily AI-news podcast with an RSS feed placed in the Library, dated two days early — [nocode.mba review](https://www.nocode.mba/articles/meta-muse-ai-review)
- Lineage contrast: the Meta AI app's 2025 "Discover feed" was a social feed of other people's shared prompts ("a place to share and explore how others are using AI"), not a private agent-written feed — [Meta Newsroom, Apr 29 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/introducing-meta-ai-app-new-way-access-ai-assistant/)

### Inferences
- The feed is best modelled as the output of scheduled background jobs (cron-like) that write "posts" (briefing, reminder, suggestion, price-watch result) into a timeline, with lightweight reactions (heart, comment) that feed back into memory. Configuration is conversational ("tell your Muse what to write about") plus the Soul/Memory files, rather than a settings matrix.
- Likely input signals, inferred from Meta's examples and auto-connected accounts: conversation history, goals, memory claims, connected email/calendar, Instagram/Facebook saves and engagement, and prior reactions; no source confirms a formal ranking model.

### Gaps
- No source documents the feed's post schema, cadence defaults, mute/hide controls, or whether comments open a side chat. No fetched source shows a feed screenshot in detail.

## Q3. How do "ideas"/suggestions and goals work (generation from conversations, surfacing, tracking)?

### Takeaway
Ideas are agent-generated suggestions derived "from goals, patterns, and past conversation," shown as onboarding tips, as cards in the Ideas tab (tap → "Let's do it" popup with a recurring-task toggle), and as proactive chat suggestions tied to goals. ## Q4. How are long-running agent tasks, approvals, connected accounts, notifications (and privacy controls) shown in the UI?

### Takeaway
Tasks run in a per-user cloud VM and keep going when the app is closed; progress is shown with progressive disclosure (animated avatar + one-line status snippet → task list with per-task action log → raw logs), a status pill during browser work, and a viewable/takeover-able browser. Approvals are deterministic UI (structured accept/reject cards delivered in-app or by notification, with "Allow once / for this task / for this site / Always / Deny" scopes) issued by a separate Sentinel process, never as chat text. Connectors are added one at a time with per-connector read vs. act scopes; credentials live in a Secure Storage the agent cannot read; payments use single-use Link cards with approval each time.

## Q5. What design principles does Meta describe (and reviewers observe): tone, visual style, motion, persona customization?

### Takeaway
Meta's stated principle is "How would this work if you were talking to another human?": a WhatsApp-like single persistent conversation with a customizable, animated creature avatar at the top (default "Jolly," cream-colored), restrained proactivity, progressive disclosure of agent activity, deterministic UI only where trust requires it (approvals, credentials), and "generative UI" where card structure is fixed but content is personalized. Persona is fully user-defined (name, creature, vibe/tagline, Soul file for tone), and Meta's own instructions tell the agent "You are not a chatbot."

### Cited Findings
